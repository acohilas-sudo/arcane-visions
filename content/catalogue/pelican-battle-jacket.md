Created as a one-of-one wearable artwork on premium black denim, hand-painted with the Pelican in Her Piety.

Across the shoulders runs the motto *IGNE NATURA RENOVATUR INTEGRA*. Beneath it, the Pelican — crowned and ringed in sacred flame — wounds her own breast to feed her young, set upon the Rosy Cross above the banner **INRI**. Roses climb one sleeve, a serpent the other, and checkerboard panels run the sides. There is one, and only one.

## Artist & Craftsmanship

The jacket is the work of Debbi Cohilas. A graduate of the California Institute of the Arts, she has spent more than thirty years in film and television as a storyboard artist, director, and animation timer — her credits include *Avatar: The Last Airbender*, *Family Guy*, and *King of the Hill*. That discipline — the practised translation of an idea into a clear, weighted image — is present in every inch of this piece.

The imagery is hand-painted in professional acrylics, with high-quality heat-applied vinyl elements and rhinestone accents introduced with restraint — catching light where the composition asks for it and nowhere it does not — and sealed for the realities of wear.

## Symbolism & Meaning

The motto and the letters beneath the Pelican are the same idea twice told. *Igne Natura Renovatur Integra* — "by fire, nature is renewed whole" — is the alchemical reading of **INRI**, understood within the Rosicrucian current as a formula of purification and rebirth through fire; the sacred flames around the crowned centre are its visual echo.

The **Pelican in Her Piety** is among the oldest emblems of the tradition — self-sacrifice, nourishment drawn from one's own substance, the giving of life through wounding. She rests upon the **Rosy Cross**, the union of rose and cross from which the Rosicrucian path takes its name. The **serpent** along one sleeve carries its old alchemical office of transformation and renewal; the **roses** along the other open as the soul is said to open upon the cross of discipline. The **checkerboard** panels are the mosaic pavement — the ground of light and dark on which such work is done.
